AGENTIC AI Rehlat · Flights

Flights Watch Tower — real-time fare-anomaly agent

Program lead · Scrum delivery with AI engineers & external fare suppliers

ProblemIn online flight retail, fares move constantly across dozens of suppliers. Stale fares, sudden drops and sold-out inventory reach the customer as failed bookings, margin leakage and lost trust. Manual monitoring can't keep pace with real-time movement across every route and supplier.

Approach & decisionsI scoped it as an agentic monitoring system — a "watch tower" that continuously observes fare feeds and acts (auto-blocks anomalous fares) rather than just alerting a human too late. The core judgment call was the guardrail: block aggressively enough to protect the customer, but hold fare jumps below a 1% tolerance so the agent doesn't over-block valid fares and starve inventory. I chose bounded auto-blocking over human-in-the-loop-per-fare because latency mattered at scale, and validated against real supplier edge cases sprint by sprint.

OutcomeEliminated stale fares, uncontrolled fare drops and sold-outs from reaching customers, with fare jumps held under the 1% threshold.

Fare jumps <1% Stale fares eliminated Delivery Scrum sprints
MACHINE LEARNING Rehlat · Pricing

Markup optimization — pricing ancillary fees with ML

Product/program lead · model selection with data science

ProblemMarkup on check-in ancillary and convenience fees was set by flat, heuristic rules. Many airline-route-trip-type combinations came out unprofitable — below a $10 net-profit floor — while others were priced past what customers would pay.

Approach & decisionsI defined the business metric first — the share of combinations clearing $10 net profit — so the model was optimized against money, not just error rate. We benchmarked Linear Regression (an interpretable baseline) against Random Forest and CatBoost regressors. My call as the product owner was to choose on business-metric lift and operational fit — inference cost and explainability to commercial stakeholders — rather than the lowest RMSE alone.

OutcomeThe share of profitable airline-route-trip-type combinations rose from 60% to 85%.

Profitable mix 60% → 85% Benchmarked LinReg · RF · CatBoost
GENAI · EVALS Rehlat · Support

AI support chatbot — quality gated by evaluation

End-to-end owner · design → build → UAT → monitoring

ProblemRoutine travel queries — booking status, changes, policies — needed deflection at volume. But on a fare rule or a change fee, a wrong answer is worse than no answer, so quality couldn't be judged on vibes.

Approach & decisionsI treated quality as an evaluation problem. We built an eval framework with explicit edge-case categories — ambiguous intents, policy corner cases, multi-turn context — and an accuracy rubric, then used it as the release gate through UAT and as a live monitoring signal afterward. Defining "accuracy" concretely and making it the gate meant the bot only expanded once it cleared the bar.

OutcomeReached 95% accuracy through edge-case validation and evaluation frameworks.

Accuracy 95% Release gate eval framework
MT · HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP Rehlat · Localisation

Localisation pipeline — five markets, fast and accurate

Launch owner · pipeline & QA design

ProblemLaunching five market domains (FR, ES, CA, DE, MX) meant localising large volumes of customer-facing travel content fast — but raw machine translation risks quality, brand and legal errors that damage trust in a new market.

Approach & decisionsI designed a machine-translation-plus-human-in-the-loop pipeline: MT for speed and coverage, a human QA gate for accuracy and brand/legal safety. The trade-off was explicit — pure human translation is accurate but slow and costly; pure MT is fast but risky. HITL captured most of the speed while holding a 99% accuracy bar across all five markets.

OutcomeCut localisation turnaround by 50% at 99% accuracy.

How I work

Requirements

Writing the PRD

Every program starts with a spec a data scientist, an engineer and a commercial sponsor can all act on — not a wish-list.

  • Prioritise use-cases on value, feasibility and data-readiness before any build — the CPMAI discipline of proving the data can actually support the model.
  • Define the success metric in the PRD up front, as a business metric (net profit cleared, accuracy at the release gate) — not an offline score.
  • Cut scope to an MVP with explicit acceptance criteria, so UAT has something objective to sign off against.
Delivery

Sprint planning & cadence

I've run 20+ concurrent Agile initiatives on a single cadence and delivered 90% of them on time.

  • Backlog grooming → sprint planning → daily standups → retrospectives → release planning, tracking velocity and burndown in Jira.
  • Hybrid by design: Agile/Scrum for uncertain model & experimentation work; stage-gated planning for integration, compliance and go-live where the dates are fixed.
  • Run dependencies and risk from a live RAID log, resolving conflicts before they reach the critical path.
Judgment

Making the call

The hard part of AI delivery isn't the model — it's the judgment around it.

  • Anchor on the business metric: I chose the markup model on profit lift and explainability, not the lowest error rate.
  • Set the autonomy boundary deliberately: bounded auto-blocking for fares where latency rules; human-in-the-loop QA where a brand or legal error costs more.
  • Own the escalation: single point of accountability to C-level sponsors, framing the trade-offs so a decision can actually be made.
